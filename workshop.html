Workshop

Finding things, fixing things, and letting them go again.

Three verbs, and they’re all the same one.

Rescue

Thrifting is a filtering problem with bad tooling. Everything is one-of-one, nothing is labelled properly, and the only index is your own eyes going along a rail.

What I’m looking for is the gap between what something is and what it looks like right now. A good piece under a bad finish is worth more than a mediocre piece in good shape, because the first one is a problem I can solve and the second one is just a purchase.

Renew

Stripping, sanding, repairing, refinishing. Some of it is furniture, some of it is smaller craft work. The part I like is that the object tells you immediately whether you were right — there’s no arguing with a surface.

Resell

Then it goes back out — Depop, eBay, Mercari, depending on what it is and who’s looking. Pricing is the interesting half. Get it wrong high and it sits there telling you that you were wrong; get it wrong low and you find out instantly.

Where the hands came from

I dual-enrolled in manufacturing engineering at De Anza while I was still in high school. CAD and SolidWorks, 3D printing across FDM, VAT and powder-bed fusion, CNC machining, and manual metalwork — designing parts and then actually making them, end to end.

That’s the part that makes the rest of this page a habit rather than a hobby. Once you’ve machined something to a tolerance, a wobbly chair stops looking like a chair and starts looking like a fixable joint.

Which is why I built

Resell Assistant

Pricing by feel works until you’re holding forty things at once.

So I wrote an MCP server I can ask in plain language what something is worth — and which keeps the financial and inventory tracker in Google Sheets current on its own, instead of me updating a spreadsheet at midnight.
